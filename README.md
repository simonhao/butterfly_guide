# 🦋 Butterfly Atlas

> A curated, interactive atlas of **158 butterfly species** from around the world — explore them through a living taxonomic tree, dual-sided 3D wing models, and an immersive viewer that flaps, rests, and reveals each butterfly's hidden underside.

> ⚠️ **Notice on 3D models** — The 3D wing textures in this atlas were produced with **AI assistance** (with a few iconic species using real Wikimedia museum specimens). They are intended **for personal interest and educational visualization only** — **not for academic, taxonomic, or scientific reference**. For rigorous study, please consult primary lepidopteran literature.

---

## What's Inside

### 158 Species, Five Families
A carefully curated collection spanning the five great butterfly families:

- **Nymphalidae** — Brush-footed butterflies (Morphos, Owls, Heliconians, Fritillaries, Tigers, the Diaethria "88")
- **Papilionidae** — Swallowtails and birdwings (Ulysses, Paris Peacock, Queen Alexandra's, Kaiser-i-Hind, Apollo)
- **Pieridae** — Whites, sulphurs, jezebels, orange-tips
- **Lycaenidae** — Blues, hairstreaks, coppers
- **Hesperiidae** — Skippers

Every scientific name has been cross-checked against authoritative taxonomic registries.

### Dual-Sided 3D Wing Models
Open any species in the 3D viewer and you get more than a flat picture:

- **Real dorsal and ventral textures** on every model — not a mirror, the actual underside pattern
- **Live flapping animation** — toggle on to watch the wings beat
- **Free orbit** — drag to rotate, scroll to zoom
- **Auto-rotate** — sit back and watch the butterfly turn under studio lighting
- **Flip face** — switch between the topside spectacle and the cryptic underside in one click

### Resting Pose for Mimicry Masters
Four of nature's most famous mimics include a second model — **wings folded, at rest** — set against an environment that reveals their disguise:

| Species | Resting reveal | Atmosphere |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian Leaf** *Kallima inachus* | Dead-leaf venation across the closed wings | Warm amber forest floor |
| **Owl Butterfly** *Caligo memnon* | Giant owl-eye spot on the hindwing underside | Deep tropical tree-bark gloom |
| **89'er / 88 Butterfly** *Diaethria clymena* | Black "88" numerals on a white ground | Misty rainforest green |
| **Autumn Leaf** *Doleschallia bisaltide* | Perfect dry-leaf mimicry with central midrib | Soft fallen-leaf brown |

Each species also opens directly to its most striking face — a leaf-side, an owl-eye, or a textbook number — rather than the flashy side you've already seen on the cover.

### Living Taxonomic Tree
A complete seven-rank Linnaean hierarchy you can actually read:

> **Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

- The four upper ranks (Animalia · Arthropoda · Insecta · Lepidoptera) sit at the top as a clean breadcrumb
- Families are collapsible — click a header to fold the whole group
- Genera group their species into compact, scrollable chips
- Each chip shows a thumbnail, the common name, and the species epithet
- Numbers update live: "X genera · Y species" beneath every family name

### Featured Filter
A single ✨ toggle at the top of the tree narrows everything down to **36 hand-picked icons** — the species you'd want to show a friend first:

- Brilliant blue Morphos (including *Morpho rhetenor helena*, the "Sunset Morpho")
- The transparent Glasswing
- The Indian Leaf in all its dead-leaf perfection
- The migratory Monarch
- Multiple Birdwings — Queen Alexandra's, Goliath, Rajah Brooke's, Cairns
- The Kaiser-i-Hind, China's golden treasure
- The Owl Butterfly with its fearsome eyespots
- The Apollo of high alpine meadows
- … and more across every family

The tree, the chips, the cards — everything narrows in sync. Empty genera and families simply vanish, so the result is always tight and scannable.

### Detailed Species Cards
Click any thumbnail and a full panel slides in:

- **Cover photograph** with a one-tap shortcut to the 3D viewer
- **Common name · scientific name · English name · family**
- **Wingspan**
- **Distribution map** — every region the species inhabits is marked on a real world atlas with hexagonal locality pins
- **Description** — a concise scientific introduction
- **Highlights** — three bullets capturing what makes the butterfly remarkable

### Multi-Language Interface
The atlas speaks four languages out of the box:

🇨🇳 **简体中文** · 🇬🇧 **English** · 🇯🇵 **日本語** · 🇪🇸 **Español**

The interface auto-detects your browser language on first visit and remembers your choice afterwards. Every label, button, rank name, region, and 3D control is translated. Scientific names — the universal currency of biology — stay in Latin throughout, so a viewer can identify any species regardless of which language they're reading.

### Real-World Distribution Map
For every species, a stylized world map shows exactly where it flies:

- **19 distinct biogeographic regions** — from Western North America to East Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa to New Guinea
- Hexagonal markers placed at real coordinates
- Region labels are translated into your chosen language

### Authentic Sources
- **Photographs** drawn from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons under Creative Commons licenses
- **Descriptions** synthesized from Wikipedia entries and standard lepidopteran references
- **Taxonomy** cross-checked against Catalogue of Life and GBIF
- **Iconic species** (Cypris Morpho, the Indian Leaf, etc.) use **real museum specimen photos** rather than generated imagery, preserving the exact patterns that distinguish them
